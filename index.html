    Services Capability Matrix

    👤My Role: Solo Project

    Problem
    New sellers struggle with finding a centralized reference and view of all the Dell Services solutions.
    What I Delivered
    A centralized services matrix containing all the Dell Services, what customer pain-point a service addresses, and how well it addresses it
    Measurable Impact
    Reduced my personal services search time by 30% Increased visibility of services capabilities Presented to 5+ SSR
    Context & Problem

    As someone new to learning about the Dell Services portfolio, sometimes I felt like there was such an extensive list of offerings and I had nowhere to quickly reference them. I created this Matrix to enable myself and new sellers with a centralized view of the services offerings and what customer need it addresses.

    Lessons Learned

    Pitching a personal project is far different from pitching a services offering. During the first few times I shared this project, I struggled to piece together an engaging approach. However, with time, I realized that a more casual and interactive approach kept things far more engaging. I would also the build process differently. Instead of prompting the entire build from scratch in a single chain, I would seperate the process by front end UI and UX and then from that template I would upload it into another chat and make edits using AI. This process would have saved me from accidental mishaps or misprompts.

    AI Use Case Explorer

    👥My Role (Team Effort): Solo Project

    Problem
    Customer success stories lack a structure filtering system to enable new sellers with a quick reference for customer calls.
    What I Delivered
    A centralized view of real customer success stories pulled from the Dell page with an improved filtering system for industry, AI stage, and infrastructure platform.
    Measurable Impact
    Improved search time by 95%. Enabled sellers to find more relevant stories. Presented to the customer success and marketing team for Services.
    Context & Problem

    During the AI pitch week, I noticed that the customer scenario talks about a customer looking to learn about different use cases with AI. As I was looking for customer success stories to use for the week's pitch, I noticed there was a lack of a filtering system on the Dell success stories page. With this in mind, if I was a customer on that page I would hate to have to click through every single one in order to find a story that is relevant to my interest, use-case, or industry. So I made this centralized view of all the real Dell success stories with a refined filtering experience for AI-use cases, industry, stage (crawl, walk, run), and where the infrastruture is at.

    Lessons Learned

    Honestly, this whole project started because I got frustrated clicking through dozens of success stories trying to find one that was actually relevant to my pitch. That taught me that sometimes the best ideas come from just paying attention to what's annoying you in the moment instead of waiting for someone to hand you a project. When I presented it to the customer success and marketing teams, I quickly realized I had to adjust how I talked about it depending on who was in the room. Sellers cared about finding stories faster, marketing cared about how it could improve the customer browsing experience. Same tool, different angle. The biggest takeaway though was that getting feedback early genuinely changed the end result. Some of the filter categories I originally picked made sense to me but didn't match how sellers actually think during calls. That input made the tool way more useful than anything I would have built on my own.

    Cybersecurity Learning Hub - For Cyber Challenge

    👤My Role: Solo Project

    Problem
    My team and I had to learn 47 cybersecurity terms for the cyber challenge.
    What I Delivered
    I delivered a centralized tool for my team to learn all 47 terms, with simple, moderate, and in-depth explanations for each, and relevant reputable sources with hyperlinks for each term. In addition, users can track progress with the topics learned bar at the top.
    Measurable Impact
    Saved my team 2+ hours of search time researching topics. Successful Kahoot Cyber challenge team performance. Centralized view of all 47 cybersecurity terms to enable learning.
    Context & Problem

    Our team was preparing for the Cyber Challenge, which required us to learn and understand 47 cybersecurity terms. That's a lot of ground to cover, and the problem wasn't just the volume, it was how scattered the information was. Everyone was off doing their own Googling, finding different sources of varying quality, and there was no shared understanding of how deep we actually needed to go on each topic. Some terms are straightforward enough to grasp in a sentence, others really need context to click. On top of that, there was no easy way to track what we'd already covered versus what still needed attention. It felt inefficient for every person on the team to independently spend hours piecing together the same information, so I decided to build one centralized hub that everyone could learn from, with simple, moderate, and in-depth explanations depending on how comfortable someone already was with a topic, plus reputable sources linked out for anyone who wanted to go deeper. I also added a progress tracker at the top so we could see at a glance how far along we were.

    Lessons Learned

    The biggest thing I took away from this was that how you organize information matters just as much as the information itself. All 47 terms were already out there on the internet, the problem was never access, it was structure. Building the tiered explanation system (simple, moderate, in-depth) forced me to actually understand each term well enough to explain it at different levels, which honestly helped me learn the material better than just reading about it would have. I also learned that when you're building something for a team, you have to think about different learning styles and comfort levels. Not everyone wants to read a deep technical breakdown right away some people just need the quick version to build confidence before going further. The progress tracker ended up being more motivating than I expected too. Something as small as seeing a bar fill up kept the team engaged and gave us a shared sense of momentum heading into the challenge.
